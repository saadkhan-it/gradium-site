Gradium — User Guide

Last updated: 26 September 2026 · for app version 0.31.7

Gradium keeps your semesters, marks, GPA, deadlines, timetable and the university regulations in one place. It works offline and syncs to your account, so nothing is lost when you change phones.

Most "the app is showing me the wrong GPA" messages turn out to be something entered differently than the student expected. Each part below ends with the mistake people actually make. If you are in a hurry, read "My GPA looks wrong" first.

1. Set up your account

Sign up with your email address and a password of at least 8 characters with a letter and a number. A 6-digit code is emailed to prove the address is real; it expires in 10 minutes, and "Resend code" appears after a minute. From then on you sign in with your email and password only — no code, no waiting.

Then you fill in your name, your program and your registration number. The app asks you to confirm the registration number because it cannot be changed afterwards: it is what stops two accounts being made for the same student. Read it off your student card, digit by digit, before you tap confirm.

Forgot your password? "Forgot password" emails a code, then you type the new password twice — or tap Continue to Account to get in with the code and keep your old password. Changing your password from Settings → Account also needs a code, so that a phone left unlocked is not enough to take your account over.

Mistake people make: typing the registration number in a hurry. If it is wrong, email gradium.help@gmail.com — it has to be freed by hand.

2. Put in your past results

Two ways:

  • Import your transcript. Semesters → Upload latest transcript, then pick your transcript PDF (the text PDF from the portal, not a photo). It is read on your phone and never uploaded. The app reads each semester, course, credit hours and grade, and links a repeated course to the earlier attempt by itself. A grade printed as (D) or (F) is read as bracketed, exactly as your transcript shows it.
  • Add them by hand. Semesters → add a semester, set it to Completed, then add each course with its final grade.

Check the imported semesters once before trusting the CGPA: the portal's layout changes now and then, and a course the app could not read is better fixed on day one.

Mistake people make: importing the transcript and also adding the same semester by hand, so courses are counted twice. Import first, then look before adding anything.

3. Your current semester

You may have one Current semester at a time. Add your courses to it with the right credit hours; the section field is only for matching your timetable.

When the term ends, use End Semester. It turns your calculated grades into official ones, so they count in the CGPA — the app blocks it until every course has a grade. Reopen as Current does the reverse: grades are cleared and the marks boxes come back, so use it only if you ended the semester too early.

Type the course name and code exactly as your portal or transcript writes them. That is how the app recognises a repeat of an earlier attempt, and matches your class timetable and date sheet. Pick from this term's offering in Add Course fills in the name and code for you — then choose the credit hours. A course you name your own way still works, but without its code it may not line up with those.

Mistake people make: leaving last term as Current. Until you end it, its courses stay predictions, and the Completed CGPA leaves them out.

4. Marks and the breakdown

Each course has a breakdown: the groups your teacher grades you on — quizzes, assignments, midterm, final — with a weight each. The weights must add up to 100%, and no weight may be empty or negative; the app refuses to save otherwise.

Inside a group you add items (Quiz 1, Quiz 2 …) with their total marks, and you enter what you obtained as you get them back.

A group can be set to "Best of" — "best 3 of 4 quizzes", for example. The lowest entered marks are dropped, and an item you have not entered yet always counts, so the app never asks you to score marks in a quiz it has already dropped.

An item with no marks entered is assumed to be full marks. That is why a brand-new course shows 100.0 and an A: it is a prediction of what happens if everything goes perfectly, not a grade you have. It changes as you enter real marks.

Mistake people make: reading the A on a fresh course as a result. Nothing you have not entered has happened yet.

5. What the numbers mean

  • Predicted grade — worked out from the marks you entered plus full marks for everything else. Current-semester courses show this.
  • Official grade — the grade the university gave. The Completed CGPA counts only these.
  • Semester GPA — your current semester, using predicted grades, so it moves as you enter marks.
  • CGPA — the Completed / Current switch on Home and Progress decides which. Completed: finished semesters only, so it does not move while you enter marks. Current: your current semester counts too, using its predicted grades. The app remembers your choice.
  • Credit hours — the Home card shows four numbers: attempted (every course except W), earned (finished courses that count in the CGPA, F included), accepted (finished and passed — what counts toward your degree) and in progress (this semester, shown only on Current). So 35 / 137 means 35 are done.
  • The small arrow next to the GPA shows what your last marks entry did to it. It disappears after a day.

Your grade comes from the university's relative grading: the highest mark in the class and the grade gap decide the boundaries. Both can be set per course with the Grading button on the course screen, and Settings → Academic can apply your values to every course of the current semester at once.

Mistake people make: reading the Current CGPA as your real one. It includes predicted grades; switch to Completed for the CGPA you have actually earned.

6. Deadlines and reminders

Add a deadline to any item — an assignment, a quiz, a project. The Schedule tab lists the current semester's deadlines, overdue ones first; a past semester's deadlines stay with that semester (More → Progress).

Reminders are scheduled on the phone, so they arrive with the app closed. In Settings you control the master switch, the reminder types, and quiet hours (11 PM – 7 AM by default): a reminder that falls inside quiet hours is delivered when they end, or just before they start if the deadline itself is sooner.

If a reminder never arrives, check in this order: the master switch in Settings, quiet hours, and then Android's own notification permission for Gradium.

Mistake people make: expecting reminders on a second phone. They are scheduled on the phone where you added the deadline.

7. Repeats, brackets, W / I / P

  • Repeat — link the new attempt to the old one (Repeat of… when you add the course, or later on the course screen) and the app applies the university rule: a repeated F is removed from the CGPA, and when you repeat a course you had passed, the higher of the two grades counts. The old attempt is tagged Replaced.
  • Bracket — your choice to exclude a bad grade from the CGPA. The course must then be repeated, and a bracket is permanent (Regulation 12(4)): the only undo is deleting the course and adding it again. Test it first with "Bracket what-if" in the Calculator.
  • W (withdrawn), I (incomplete) and P (pass) do not count towards the GPA. An I is flagged on your dashboard until you replace it.
Mistake people make: bracketing to "clean up" a grade. It is permanent and the course still has to be repeated.

8. Timetable and date sheet

Your class timetable and the midterm and final date sheets are published centrally — you do not type them in. When a new one is published, the app tells you and the changed rows carry an "Updated" tag for three days. Overlapping classes or exams are tagged "Clash", as a warning only.

They are matched to your courses by course code (or by name when a course has no code) and section. If a class of yours is missing, check the course code and section first, then tell me.

8a. When a course is called something else

The university renames courses from time to time, so your transcript and this term's timetable may spell the same course differently. Gradium matches courses by their code: a transcript's Software Engineering-I and this term's Software Engineering share the code SE2223, so the app treats them as one course.

What to do:

  • Always fill in the course code, copied from your portal or transcript. With the code, a renamed course still links to its repeat and finds its classes and exams.
  • Without a code, the app can only compare names, so type the name exactly as your portal writes it.

This never affects your results: marks, GPA and CGPA do not depend on names or codes. It only affects repeat links, course history and timetable matching.

9. Syncing and a second phone

Everything is saved on the phone first, so the app works with no internet. When you are online it syncs to your account, and the sync badge on the dashboard shows Synced, Syncing or Offline.

On a second phone, sign in with the same email and your data arrives. If you enter different marks on two phones between syncs, both are kept — each mark is merged on its own, and only a mark you changed on both phones takes the newer one.

Files you attach stay on the phone you added them on. They are not uploaded, so on another phone the item shows a note that the file is not there.

Logging out clears the app on that phone and cancels its reminders; your data stays in your account. Attached files stay on the phone too, ready for when you sign in again.

10. "My GPA looks wrong"

Work down this list — one of these is nearly always the answer:

  1. Is last term still Current? End it, or its courses stay predictions (the Completed CGPA leaves them out; the Current one counts them as predictions). Also check which way the Completed / Current switch is set.
  2. Does every course have the right credit hours? A 3 CH course entered as 1 changes everything.
  3. Is a course in there twice? Usually a transcript import plus the same semester added by hand.
  4. Do your breakdown weights add up to 100%? A course with the wrong weights scores nothing like your class result.
  5. Is the highest mark and grade gap right for that course? Relative grading follows the class, not a fixed percentage.
  6. Is a repeat linked? Without the link, both attempts count.
  7. Are the marks entered as marks, not percentages? 8 out of 10, not 80 out of 10.
  8. Blank items are assumed full marks, so an untouched course always looks like an A.

If none of those explains it, send me a screenshot of the course and of the semester — that is usually enough to see it.
